# Deviations (physics simplifications)

Each entry: what is simplified, why, expected error, and the milestone that may remove it.

_Each entry names the milestone or audit round that introduced it; "implemented" marks the ones that were planned before they were built. DV-L2 is confirmed by the owner (D-141)._

- **DV-P1 (M3):** conical/cylindrical shadow model only; no Earth oblateness or atmospheric refraction in eclipse timing.
- **DV-P2 (M4, implemented):** the array cell temperature is one configured constant for the whole array and scenario, not a thermal model and not per mode or phase; expected error grows with the temperature swing between sunlight and eclipse. M4b may later supply temperatures.
- **DV-P3 (M5):** rain and gas attenuation come only from user-supplied tables; no built-in ITU-R P.618 implementation until a source is approved (implemented as DV-L1).
- **DV-M1 (M2b):** margin mass is not given a position, so the centre of gravity and inertia use nominal (unmargined) masses; the margin appears only in the mass totals. Inertia margins are not applied.
- **DV-M2 (M2b):** units are rigid bodies described by a mass, a position and an inertia tensor; flexible appendages, deployed configurations and moving parts (reaction wheel angular momentum) are not modelled; deployed and stowed states are handled only as separate phases.
- **DV-T1 (M4b, implemented):** lumped, steady-state thermal model: isothermal nodes, linear conductances, radiation to space with user-given radiator areas and optical properties; no radiative view factors between nodes, no orbital transient, no internal convection or fluid loops.
- **DV-T2 (M4b, implemented):** environment heat loads are user-supplied constants per case, not computed from orbit geometry (a later milestone may derive them from the M3 environment).
- **DV-M3 (M2b):** items are rigid bodies with a fixed centre of mass; fuel slosh, propellant centre-of-mass shift inside a tank and deployed versus stowed geometry are not modelled (since 2.2.0 an expendable may give the position of its centre of mass for each phase, `phase_positions_m`, D-230: the centre of mass inside a tank is then yours to give, the tool does not compute it from the fill level). Inertia margins are not applied (DV-M1).
- **DV-E1 (M3):** Earth rotation uses GMST only (UTC treated as UT1, error up to 0.9 s, about 400 m on the ground; no polar motion, no nutation of the Earth-fixed frame); the Sun uses low-precision formulae (about 0.01 degree, eclipse edge timing error of a few seconds at most); no atmospheric refraction in elevation. All eclipse and pass times carry these limits.
- **DV-E2 (M3):** elements given by the user are used as SGP4 mean elements; the semi-major axis becomes mean motion by Kepler's third law (the SGP4 initialisation then applies its own mean-element conversion, a difference of order 0.1 percent in period); no drag (B* = 0); Earth orbits only.
- **DV-E4 (audit, short shadows found after the release, D-146):** passes and eclipses are detected on a fixed 20 s grid, whatever `step_s`. A pass that rises above the minimum elevation between two samples is found from the local maximum of the elevation, so passes of a few seconds are found; an eclipse or an umbra shorter than the grid (a grazing shadow) is found in the same way, from the local maximum of how far inside the shadow the satellite is (D-146), so shadows of a few seconds are found. What is not looked for is a gap of sunlight shorter than the grid between two shadows (a single orbit does not produce one), and a shadow that crosses a grid sample in imported (SpaceMissionStudio) data is taken from that data as it is. Pass and eclipse edges are bisected to about 1 ms; the soft shadow is a linear ramp over each penumbra (DV-P6).
- **DV-E5 (audit V2, D-217):** the gravity constants differ by how the orbit is given. A TLE is propagated with the SGP4 library's default set, **WGS-72** (Earth radius 6378.135 km, mu 398600.8 km3/s2), the usual pairing for TLEs; orbital elements are converted with the **WGS-84** set of the same library (6378.137 km, 398600.5 km3/s2); and the geometry of the shadow, the sites and the link uses the WGS-84 radius whichever way the orbit was given. The difference is 2 m in the radius (3e-7 of it) and 0.3 km3/s2 in mu (8e-7 of it), far below the other errors of this model, but it is a choice the user cannot make: the choice is now the `earth_model` of the scenario (WGS-84 by default, or WGS-72; a TLE stays on WGS-72, D-221). The radius of the static link budget (spherical Earth for pointing, DV-L2) stays WGS-84, and the altitude-to-semi-major-axis conversion of the forms uses the WGS-84 radius.
- **DV-P2 (2.2.0, D-234):** the slew time is that of a rigid body turning about one principal axis with a constant torque that reverses instantly at the half-way point (bang-bang), optionally limited in rate: no flexible modes or sloshing, no coupling between axes or gyroscopic torque, no actuator dynamics, saturation curve or momentum exchange between wheels, no jerk limit, and a slew is about one body axis only. The inertia from the mass budget counts only items with a position, so it is a lower bound and the slew it gives is optimistic. Holding and slewing power are inputs, not derived from the torque.
- **DV-P1 (2.2.0, D-233):** the propulsion budget treats every manoeuvre as an impulsive burn with the delta-v you give: no gravity loss, steering loss or finite-burn loss, no thrust or Isp that varies (blow-down, throttling), no residual or unusable propellant (add it to the dry mass), and no propellant for attitude control unless it is listed as a manoeuvre. The burn time uses a constant thrust. An electric system's power is an input, not derived from the thrust and the specific impulse. The standard gravity is the 1901 definition.
- **DV-E6 (2.2.0, D-226):** the orbit design helpers (`budget orbit`) use the first-order secular J2 relations, not the theory SGP4 uses (which has higher orders). Against SGP4 the node rate of a designed orbit differs by 0.15 % (0.9841 against 0.9856 °/day for a sun-synchronous design at 650 km), so its local time moves by 11 s in 30 days; a repeat ground track of 14 revolutions a day closes to 0.21° of longitude against tracks 25.7° apart; the secular rates differ from SGP4's by up to 0.2 % (node), 0.2 % (perigee) and 0.06 % (mean anomaly). The elements are mean elements (DV-E2); the node of the osculating orbit at the epoch is about 0.03 h (two minutes) of local time from the mean one. The local time is the mean solar time, without the equation of time (up to ±16 minutes in the year), and UTC stands for UT1 (DV-E1). No drag, third-body force or frozen-orbit condition.
- **DV-E7 (2.2.0, D-229):** an orbit given as a state vector is made into mean elements before it is propagated. (1) The frame: J2000 to TEME is the IAU-76 precession, the ten largest terms of the IAU-1980 nutation and the equation of the equinoxes; against the full GCRS to TEME transformation of astropy it differs by 0.7 m (2000) to 2.4 m (2040) at a radius of 7000 km (the rest is the older precession model and the frame bias of 23 mas), and UTC stands for TT and UT1 here (a minute is 2e-5 arcseconds of precession). (2) The elements: the six mean elements are found for which SGP4's own state at the epoch is the given state (Newton's method, two iterations for a low orbit, six for a geostationary one, about a millisecond), so the state is reproduced at the epoch to 3e-5 m and 1e-8 m/s; after the epoch the orbit is SGP4's, which has no drag and the other simplifications of DV-E2. (3) Mean and osculating elements differ: for a 600 km orbit the semi-major axis of the mean orbit is 11.7 km below the osculating one, which is why elements copied from an osculating source and typed as `elements:` are 10 km off, and a state vector is not. A retrograde orbit in the equatorial plane (inclination 180 degrees) is not supported, and a state with a perigee below the surface is refused.
- **DV-E3 (M3):** passes are visibility above a site's minimum elevation only; tracking-rate limits and antenna keep-out zones arrive with the link budget (M5).
- **DV-P4 (M4):** the battery is an energy store with constant charge and discharge efficiencies and a capacity that fades only with age. No voltage curve, no charge or discharge current or power limit, no temperature or state-of-charge dependence, no self-discharge. The depth of discharge is `1 - E / E_cap`. Expected error: small for energy budgets, large when current limits or cold batteries dominate.
- **DV-P5 (M4):** array generation uses a constant irradiance (no Earth-Sun distance variation unless the scenario chooses `irradiance_model: seasonal` (D-222; the default is the constant irradiance), no albedo or Earth infrared), perfect pointing in the `sun` mode (no pointing error), and a body frame equal to the local orbital frame in the `nadir` mode; no shadowing of one face by another or by appendages; no power-conditioning topology beyond the configured converter efficiencies (no MPPT or direct-energy-transfer model); the bus margin is one system-level series (generation minus demand), not per bus.
- **DV-P6 (M4, audit):** the Sun direction and pointing mode are sampled at the start of each step; the demand and the sunlit share use the exact overlap with segments and eclipses, so the remaining error is the change of the cosine factor inside one step. A soft (conical) shadow is the ratio ramping linearly from 1 at the start of an eclipse to 0 at the start of its umbra and back to 1 at the end of the eclipse (taken from the eclipse and umbra intervals, so it does not depend on the step); the true penumbra is S-shaped, so the ramp overstates the sunlit time of a penumbra by about 0.1 s (an 8 s penumbra at 400 km altitude: 4.10 s against 3.99 s). An eclipse without an umbra (a grazing orbit) ramps to the deepest sampled value inside it, or to one half when no sample falls inside.
- **DV-T3 (M4b):** a surface radiates and absorbs over the same area to space only (no view factors between surfaces or to other bodies, no multi-layer insulation model other than a chosen emissivity); the Sun and Earth view ratios are constants per case, so a case is one orbital condition and not an orbit-averaged or time-dependent result; unit dissipation uses average power x duty cycle, unmargined; conduction is linear and temperature-independent. Expected error: depends on how well the cases bracket the real orbit; the model is for design trades and limit checks, not flight predictions.
- **DV-L1 (M5, implemented):** atmospheric, rain, gas, cloud and scintillation losses are single table values per elevation (no statistics, availability or exceedance probability, no climate data); path loss is free space only; no ionospheric effects.
- **DV-L2 (M5):** the Earth is a sphere for pointing angles; a spacecraft antenna boresight is at nadir; a ground antenna tracks perfectly (constant gain); pointing, polarisation and implementation losses are constants you supply; no Doppler shift, no antenna pointing error beyond the pointing loss, no interference. An inter-satellite link has static points only (no geometry source). Confirmed by the owner (D-141).
- **DV-L3 (M5, audit):** a pass is sampled in pieces and each piece is evaluated with the elevation and range at its **middle**, never outside the pass. When the environment can give the geometry at any time (the orbit propagator), the pieces are at most 5 s long, so the volume and the lowest margin do not depend on `step_s`; when it cannot (imported data), a piece is the part of the pass in each scenario step and the geometry is interpolated linearly between the grid samples. The rate is selected per sample without hysteresis, so a rate change is placed to within half a sample; no protocol overhead, link acquisition time or buffering. The data volume per day is a plain scaling of the scenario total and is given only for a scenario of one day or more.
- **DV-M4 (audit):** an expendable's own inertia tensor (`mass_properties.inertia`) is the same in every phase; it does not scale with the phase mass (a tank at 10 % of its mass keeps its full inertia). The tool warns (`MASS_INERTIA_NOT_SCALED`) when an expendable has an inertia and more than one positive mass; give such an item no own inertia (it is then a point mass), or accept the error.
- **DV-C1 (M6):** the comparison reports values as displayed (display precision hides smaller changes) and matches rows by the key columns a table declares (the first column unless it says otherwise; see D-113), so a renamed unit is one removed and one added row; it compares the report tables, not the underlying solver arrays.
- **DV-G1 (M6):** the user guide PDF has no italic face (IBM Plex Italic is not bundled), so italics print upright; the HTML shows italics.
- **DV-W1 (M6):** the wizard builds near-circular orbits only (the *Add scenario* form can also build an elliptical one from a perigee and an apogee altitude, D-196) (eccentricity 0.001) from altitude, inclination and RAAN; argument of perigee 90 deg and mean anomaly 0 as in the samples; the orbit is not checked for sun-synchronism or decay.
- **DV-R1 (audit round):** the XLSX keeps the exact number in each cell and a number format; a tiny negative number that rounds to zero (`-0.0004` with three decimals) is shown by Excel as `-0.000` although the PDF, DOCX and comparison show `0.000`. The cell is not changed because the exact value is the point of a spreadsheet.
- **DV-R3 (audit round):** pass tables of the comparison are keyed by the AOS time as displayed (to the second). A pass whose AOS moves by a second or more between two revisions is one removed and one added row.
- **DV-R4 (audit round):** a PDF shortens table cells above 400 characters and shows characters its font lacks as `?`; a plot does the same for labels. The other formats keep the full text.

- **DV-I1 (audit round):** a pass imported with `budget scenario` from the interim SpaceMissionStudio format has no `time_of_max_s` unless the optional `profile_<site>.csv` exists; with it, the time is the profile sample with the highest elevation inside the pass, so it is only as precise as the profile is dense. The interim format has no column for it (D-124).
- **DV-IDM1 (2.4.0, D-240):** the IDM-CIC model gives one power value per power mode; the import uses it as the average and as the peak power of the mode (the peak is marked `estimate`). The peak-power checks are therefore no more conservative than the model. Heat share = dissipation / power; for a mode with no power and no dissipation it is 1.0 (the heat is zero either way).
- **DV-IDM2 (2.4.0, D-240):** an equipment with no power mode in the model is imported with one mode, *Passive*, at 0 W (marked `estimate`): the model treats equipment without power modes as not consuming power.
- **DV-IDM3 (2.4.0, D-240):** the propellant of a tank is capacity x filling of the configuration. The format description says the capacity is the propellant mass at the maximal filling, and the models fill tanks up to 1 (even when the maximal filling is 0.95), so the filling is read as a share of that maximal fill. This reading is unconfirmed against a figure of IDM-CIC itself.
- **DV-IDM4 (2.4.0, D-240):** margins of an element (mass, power) and the forced mass of a subsystem or of the propellant have no counterpart in the project; they are listed, not applied. The way IDM-CIC combines the levels is not in the format description, so the project's totals can differ from IDM-CIC's by those margins.
- **DV-IDM5 (2.5.0, D-245):** the centre of gravity and the inertia of an imported unit are worked out with placement and shape conventions that were inferred from the models and tested by closing the platforms of the models into boxes, not taken from IDM-CIC. If one is wrong the error is systematic (every part with two or three rotations, or every shape of a kind, misplaced alike). Mass is unaffected. The values are marked `estimate`; use `--mass-properties none` to leave them out, or replace a unit's mass properties by hand (the import keeps your value on the next import).
